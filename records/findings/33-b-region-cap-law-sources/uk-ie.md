# Resale price law: England and Wales, Scotland, Northern Ireland, Republic of Ireland

Retrieved 2026-11-08 (UTC). Descriptive only, no advice, no recommendation.
Every statement below is tied to the primary source named beside it. Where this run
could not establish something from a primary source, it is listed under **Unknowns**
rather than filled by inference.

## What is law vs announced

| Jurisdiction | Price cap in force? | Instrument that would carry it | Status today |
|---|---|---|---|
| GB-EAW | No | Consumer Rights Act 2015 ss.90-95 (disclosure, platform duties, civil penalty) | In force. No price cap anywhere in it. |
| GB-EAW | No (announced only) | Government commitment of 19 Nov 2025: resale above "original ticket price plus unavoidable fees" to be made illegal, resale-platform fee cap, platform duty to monitor, purchase-limit ban, CMA penalties up to 10% of global turnover | **Announced policy. Not law.** No bill or instrument giving effect to it was identified. |
| GB-SCT | No | Same GB-wide CRA 2015 chapter; no Scottish instrument of its own | In force (CRA), no separate Scottish cap. No Holyrood bill on resale identified. |
| GB-NIR | No | Same UK-wide CRA 2015 chapter, enforced in NI by the Department | In force (CRA), no separate NI cap. |
| IE | **Yes** | Sale of Tickets (Cultural, Entertainment, Recreational and Sporting Events) Act 2021, s.15 | In force since 31 July 2021, but only for events in **designated venues** and **designated events** |

Two things sit behind that table and matter for a pricing engine: the UK's only
in-force price-related prohibition on tickets is the football-specific one in
s.166 of the Criminal Justice and Public Order Act 1994 (which is an *authorisation*
rule, not a price rule, and is confined to designated football matches in England
and Wales), and Ireland's cap is real but territorially and subject-matter limited by
a designation mechanism.

## GB-EAW — England and Wales

**1. Law in force, or announced?** Law in force exists, but it is **not** a price cap.
The Consumer Rights Act 2015, Chapter 5 ("Secondary ticketing", ss.90-95) is in force.
A price cap is **announced policy only** (19 November 2025 government announcement).

**2. Instruments and URLs fetched**

- Consumer Rights Act 2015, ss.90-95 —
  `https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2015/15/section/90`, `/section/93`, `/section/100`
  (chapter contents read at `https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2015/15/contents`)
- Digital Economy Act 2017, s.105 (amends CRA s.90(4)) —
  `https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2017/30/section/105`; s.106 —
  `https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2017/30/section/106`
- Criminal Justice and Public Order Act 1994, s.166 —
  `https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1994/33/section/166`
- Government announcement, 19 November 2025 —
  `https://www.gov.uk/government/news/government-bans-ticket-touting-to-protect-fans-from-rip-off-prices`
  and consultation outcome page
  `https://www.gov.uk/government/consultations/putting-fans-first-consultation-on-the-resale-of-live-events-tickets`

**3. Cap basis.** None. CRA s.90 requires the seller and each operator of the
secondary ticketing facility to give the buyer, before the contract binds, the seat
or standing-area information, any restriction on use, and **"the face value of the
ticket"** (s.90(3)(c)), which s.90(5) defines as "the amount stated on the ticket as
its price". Nothing in the chapter limits what may be charged. s.91 is titled
"Prohibition on cancellation or blacklisting" and s.92 "Duty to report criminal
activity" (chapter contents page); both are duties on platforms, not price rules.

The announced policy is a different shape: the 19 November 2025 press release says
resale above face value "will be illegal – this will be defined in legislation as the
original ticket price plus unavoidable fees, including service charges", that
"service fees charged by resale platforms will be capped", that platforms "will have
a legal duty to monitor and enforce compliance with the price cap", and that
individuals will be barred from reselling more tickets than they were entitled to
buy. Those are stated in the future tense; the same page describes them as
"proposals" and "plans".

**4. Coverage.** CRA Chapter 5 applies to re-sale of a ticket for "a recreational,
sporting or cultural event in the United Kingdom" through a secondary ticketing
facility (s.90(1)); s.95 defines the facility. No designation step, no event list.
s.90(9) limits it to tickets first offered for re-sale through the facility after
commencement.

Separately, CJPOA 1994 s.166 (E+W) makes it an offence for an **unauthorised**
person to sell or otherwise dispose of a ticket for a **designated football match**,
where "designated" means designated by order of the Secretary of State (s.166(2)(c),
(2A)); s.166(6) extends the section by order to other sporting events with 6,000 or
more tickets. The designation order in force is the Ticket Touting (Designation of
Football Matches) Order 2007 (S.I. 2007/790), seen in the legislation.gov.uk title
index for "ticket" — the order itself was not fetched in this run. The rule bites on
authorisation, not on price: an authorised seller is outside it, an unauthorised one
is caught however low the price. Territorial reach of both instruments is the UK;
CRA s.93(1) and (6) let a local weights and measures authority in England and Wales
penalise a breach occurring anywhere in England and Wales.

**5. Penalty and enforcement.** CRA s.93: enforcement authorities are local weights
and measures authorities in Great Britain (s.93(1)), the Department of Enterprise,
Trade and Investment in Northern Ireland (s.93(2)), and — inserted 6 April 2025 by
the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024 ss.216(2), 339(1) — the
Competition and Markets Authority (s.93(2A)). The authority may impose a **financial
penalty** on a person who breached a duty or prohibition in the chapter, capped at
**£5,000** per breach (s.93(9)); a due-diligence defence applies to breaches of
s.90/s.91 (s.93(5)). The penalty attaches to whoever holds the duty — the seller and
each facility operator under s.90(2), not the event organiser as such. CJPOA s.166(3)
is a criminal offence punishable on summary conviction with a fine not exceeding
level 5 on the standard scale.

**6. Dates.** CRA 2015: Chapter 5 comes into force "at the end of the period of two
months beginning with the day on which this Act is passed" (s.100(4)); the Act is
2015 c.15. The exact date was not read off a commencement order in this run, so it is
left null below. Amendments: CRA s.90(4)(c)-(e) amended 6 April 2018 for E.W.S. by
DEA 2017 s.105 (S.I. 2018/382, reg. 2), adding the unique ticket number; CRA s.93
amended 6 April 2025 by DMCCA 2024 (S.I. 2025/272). DEA 2017 s.105 is in force at
6.4.2018 for E.W.S. (S.I. 2018/382 reg. 2, read on the section page). DEA 2017 s.106
is a **power** to create a bot-purchase offence; the power itself came into force
31.7.2017 for E.W.S. (S.I. 2017/765 reg. 2(ee)); the section page shows no
regulations made under it, and none were identified. CJPOA s.166(1) as it now reads
was substituted 6.4.2007 by the Violent Crime Reduction Act 2006 s.53 (S.I. 2007/858
art. 2(k)).

**7. Announced, not law.** As at 2026-11-08 no Act or instrument identified in this
run gives the November 2025 policy legal effect. A title search of legislation.gov.uk
for "ticket" returned 29 items, none of them a 2025 or 2026 Act or SI imposing a
resale price cap. A search of the parliamentary Bills API for titles containing
"ticket" returned one live bill, the Sale of Tickets (Sporting and Cultural Events)
Bill 2025-26 (bill 3793, Dr Rupa Huq, Commons, at second reading, last updated
2026-05-05), whose long title is confined to "transparency of ticket prices" — not
the government's cap, and not law.

## GB-SCT — Scotland

**1. Law in force, or announced?** Law in force exists and is the **same GB-wide
chapter** as England and Wales; it contains no price cap. No Scottish instrument
imposing a resale price cap exists in what this run could check, and no Scottish
price cap has been announced by the UK government as applying differently in
Scotland.

**2. Instruments and URLs fetched.** As GB-EAW: CRA 2015 ss.90-95
(`https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2015/15/section/93`); DEA 2017 s.105, in force
"for E.W.S." (`https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2017/30/section/105`). Scottish
bill position checked against the Parliament's own open data feed,
`https://data.parliament.scot/api/bills` (471 bills, latest references SP Bill 80 and
SP Bill 1 of the current session); no bill with "ticket", "tout", "resale" or "live
event" in its title.

**3. Cap basis.** None. Same disclosure duty as GB-EAW: face value, seat
information, use restrictions stated before contract.

**4. Coverage.** CRA s.93(1) makes a local weights and measures authority in Great
Britain — which includes Scotland — an enforcement authority, and s.93(7) lets a
Scottish authority penalise a breach occurring anywhere in Scotland. DEA 2017 s.105's
commencement entry is "for E.W.S.", so the unique-ticket-number wording added to
CRA s.90(4)(e) is in force in Scotland as part of E.W.S. The only Scotland-specific
ticket-touting instrument surfaced in the legislation.gov.uk title index is the
Glasgow Commonwealth Games Act 2008 (Ticket Touting Offence) (Scotland) Regulations
2012 (S.S.I. 2012/323) — event-specific and spent with the 2014 Games; the 2008 Act
itself was not fetched.

**5. Penalty and enforcement.** Identical to GB-EAW: civil financial penalty up to
£5,000 per breach (CRA s.93(9)), imposed by a Scottish local weights and measures
authority or the CMA.

**6. Dates.** As GB-EAW.

**7. Announced, not law in Scotland.** Nothing separate. Consumer protection is not
devolved, so a Scottish resale cap would not be a Holyrood competence in the ordinary
way; that constitutional point is not verified from a primary source in this run and
is therefore flagged, not asserted. See Unknowns.

## GB-NIR — Northern Ireland

**1. Law in force, or announced?** Law in force: the same CRA 2015 Chapter 5, which
extends to Northern Ireland and is enforced there by the Department. No NI-specific
price cap, in force or announced.

**2. Instruments and URLs fetched.** CRA 2015 s.93
(`https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2015/15/section/93`): s.93(2) names "the
Department of Enterprise, Trade and Investment" as the enforcement authority in
Northern Ireland, and s.93(2A) adds the CMA from 6.4.2025. A title search of
legislation.gov.uk for "ticket" (`https://www.legislation.gov.uk/all?title=ticket`)
returned no Act of the Northern Ireland Assembly and no Northern Ireland Order on
ticket resale.

**3. Cap basis.** None. Disclosure duty as above.

**4. Coverage.** UK-wide event definition (CRA s.90(1)); NI enforcement authority
under s.93(2). One asymmetry that matters: DEA 2017 s.105 is in force only "for
E.W.S.", so the unique ticket number requirement added to CRA s.90(4)(e) does not
apply in Northern Ireland on the face of the commencement entry read here.

**5. Penalty and enforcement.** Civil financial penalty up to £5,000 per breach
(s.93(9)), imposed by the NI Department or the CMA.

**6. Dates.** As GB-EAW; DMCCA amendment to s.93 in force 6.4.2025.

**7. Announced, not law.** The November 2025 announcement is framed UK-wide in
rhetoric ("any platform reselling tickets to UK fans") but no instrument exists yet,
and nothing indicates separate NI legislation.

## IE — Republic of Ireland

**1. Law in force, or announced?** **Law in force.** A statutory prohibition on
selling, or advertising for sale, a ticket above its original sale price, for events
in designated venues and for designated events. Commenced 31 July 2021.

**2. Instruments and URLs fetched**

- Sale of Tickets (Cultural, Entertainment, Recreational and Sporting Events) Act
  2021 (No. 21 of 2021), ss.1, 2, 6, 7, 9, 15, 23 —
  `https://www.irishstatutebook.ie/eli/2021/act/21/enacted/en/html` and
  `/section/15/enacted/en/html` (and the other section URLs of the same pattern)
- S.I. No. 398/2021, Commencement Order —
  `https://www.irishstatutebook.ie/eli/2021/si/398/made/en/html`
- S.I. No. 399/2021, Designation of Venues and Events Regulations —
  `https://www.irishstatutebook.ie/eli/2021/si/399/made/en/html`
- Act list for 2021, used to fix the act number —
  `https://www.irishstatutebook.ie/eli/isbc/2021.html`

**3. Cap basis.** The **original sale price**, which by s.2 includes fees: s.15(1)
prohibits a secondary ticket seller from selling "for a price exceeding the original
sale price", and s.2 defines that as "(a) the price for which a ticket or ticket
package was sold by a primary ticket seller and **includes any additional charge or
fee applying to the sale** of the ticket or ticket package", or (b) the price of an
equivalent ticket where the seller did not obtain it by payment. So: face value plus
the fees that applied on the primary sale, i.e. zero headroom above that figure. The
prohibition also catches advertising for sale above that price (s.15(2)).

Related duties, not price rules: s.16 (information a primary seller must give), s.17
(information a secondary seller and secondary marketplace operator must give), s.19
(a contract term purporting to exclude or limit transfer or sale at or below the
original price is void), s.18 (exemption for charitable organisations and amateur
sports clubs).

**4. Coverage.** Not all tickets. s.15 applies to a "relevant event", defined in s.2
as "an event taking place in a designated venue or a designated event". A venue is
designated under s.7 by the Minister on application by a venue operator where the
venue holds 1,000 persons or more and the applicant is of the reasonable opinion that
events there may give rise to resale above the original price; s.7(6) lets the
Minister designate a venue, including one under 1,000 capacity, without an
application after consulting the operator. An event is designated under s.9 on
application by an event organiser or venue operator on the same reasonable-opinion
test, including annual or periodic events (s.9(2)). s.14 provides for a public
register of designated venues, and s.11-13 provide for notification,
representations and appeals. S.I. 399/2021, despite its title, only prescribes the
application **forms** (Schedules 1 and 2) — it designates no venue and no event.

Reach: the offence is committed by a "secondary ticket seller", defined in s.2 as a
person who sells a ticket originally sold by or through a primary ticket seller, and
"advertise" in s.2 expressly includes advertising "by electronic communication,
including by means of the internet". There is no express territorial formula in s.15
beyond s.6, which applies the Act to sales and advertisements "on or after the day on
which this section comes into operation". Whether a platform transacting entirely
outside the State is caught is therefore not answered on the face of the Act — see
Unknowns.

**5. Penalty and enforcement.** Criminal, not administrative. s.15(3) makes
contravention of s.15(1) or (2) an offence. s.23(2): on summary conviction, a class B
fine and/or up to six months for a first offence, a class A fine and/or up to twelve
months for a second or subsequent offence; on conviction on indictment, a fine not
exceeding €100,000 and/or imprisonment up to two years. The penalty attaches to the
secondary ticket seller. Enforcement machinery is police-led: ss.20-22 give members
of An Garda Síochána powers to enter and search, an offence of obstruction, and
arrest without warrant; s.24 provides general defences and s.25 a defence for a
secondary ticket operator; s.23(4) extends liability to directors and officers of a
body corporate. Prosecutions may be brought within two years (s.23(3)). The Minister
maintains the register (s.14). Class A and class B fine amounts are set by the Fines
Act 2010, which was not fetched here.

**6. Dates.** Act dated 14 July 2021 (the enactment note on the Act page). S.I.
398/2021, made 28 July 2021, appointed **31 July 2021** as the day the whole Act came
into operation. S.I. 399/2021 also came into operation 31 July 2021. No amending Act
or further instrument was found: a sweep of the Irish Statute Book's yearly
Statutory Instrument indexes for 2022, 2023, 2024, 2025 and 2026 (all page ranges on
each index) returned **no** SI with "Sale of Tickets" in its title. Current status:
in force, unamended as far as this run could see.

**7. Unknowns.** Which venues and events are on the s.14 register today. See below.
